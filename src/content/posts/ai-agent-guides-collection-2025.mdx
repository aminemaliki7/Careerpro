## What Makes 2025 Different

### The Shift from Research to Production
These guides reflect a critical inflection point: AI agents have moved from academic papers to board-level discussions. Every guide here addresses **real deployment challenges**, not theoretical possibilities.

### Context Engineering Emerges
Notice the emphasis on "context engineering" in newer guides. The industry has realized that **prompting is dead**—the new competitive advantage is designing information contexts that enable agent autonomy.

### Multi-Agent Systems Go Mainstream
Single-agent architectures are giving way to **specialized agent networks**. The guides from 2025 reflect this architectural evolution with frameworks for orchestration and coordination.

## Common Patterns Across All Guides

### 1. **Governance is Non-Negotiable**
Every enterprise guide emphasizes compliance, auditability, and human oversight. Microsoft's governance whitepaper (#5) is required reading.

### 2. **Start Small, Scale Fast**
All consulting firms recommend pilot projects targeting specific use cases before enterprise-wide rollouts.

### 3. **Context > Prompts**
The most sophisticated guides (Anthropic #3, Context Engineering #20) focus on architectural context design rather than prompt engineering.

### 4. **Hybrid Human-Agent Teams**
No guide suggests fully autonomous agents replacing humans. The future is **augmentation, not replacement**.

## Red Flags to Avoid

### Over-Architecting Too Early
Don't start with multi-agent systems (#18) before mastering single agents. Build complexity progressively.

### Ignoring Governance
Skipping Microsoft's governance guide (#5) will create technical debt that becomes catastrophic at scale.

### Treating Agents Like APIs
Agents aren't deterministic functions. Understanding their probabilistic nature (covered in #16, #17) is critical.

### Prompt-First Thinking
If you're still thinking about "better prompts," you're behind. Context engineering (#3, #20) is the new paradigm.
