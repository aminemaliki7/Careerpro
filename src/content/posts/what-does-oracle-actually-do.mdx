## Oracle at its core: A Database Company  
I spoke with **Belle Lin**, a Wall Street Journal reporter who covers Oracle and the quieter, backend parts of tech.

Here’s the simplest explanation you’ll ever hear:

**Oracle = databases.**

Not the fun, flashy kind.  
The essential, boring, *everything-runs-on-this* kind.

Customer records. Financial transactions. Supply chain data.  
The plumbing of the digital world.

Nothing glamorous.  
But absolutely critical.

So how the hell does a “database company” end up in AI, cloud, TikTok, HR software, and half of the U.S. government?

---

## The Answer: Databases Are *Sticky*
Belle explains:

Once a company stores its data with Oracle, it's incredibly hard to move.

So Oracle realized:

- “Hey, since we already store all your data…  
what if we also sell you HR software?”

- “And supply chain software?”

- “And cloud infrastructure too?”

Thus began the Great Expansion.

## A Brief (Chaotic) History of Oracle  
To understand Oracle today, you need the origin story.

- Oracle began as a **CIA project**.  
  Code name: *Oracle*. Yes, really.

- Larry Ellison and colleagues built databases for U.S. intelligence.

- They spun out and created the company we know today.

- In the 90s, Oracle expanded into enterprise software (HR, accounting, etc.)

- 2000s: they bought companies like crazy.

- 2010s: full pivot into the cloud  
  (to compete with AWS and Google Cloud).

At every step, Oracle morphed into whatever the market required to survive.

## But Let’s Pump the Brakes  
Belle gives a critical reminder:

Much of Oracle’s hype is **future revenue**, not current profit.

And if the AI bubble bursts?

Oracle is heavily exposed.

- OpenAI can’t yet cover the cost of all the compute it’s promised  
- Circular deals raise sustainability questions  
- Data centers need land, power, political approval  
- And they still need to be monetized later  

The risks are real.

Whatever it takes to stay relevant.

From CIA databases → enterprise software → cloud → AI → TikTok.
